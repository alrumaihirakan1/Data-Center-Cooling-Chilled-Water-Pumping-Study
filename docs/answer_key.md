# Answer key

1. Each kilogram of water absorbs 4188.55 x 5 = 20,942.75 J. Dividing 1,000,000 W by this value gives **47.75 kg/s**. Divide by density to get **0.0477922 m3/s = 47.79 litres/s = 172.05 m3/h**. The heat balance sets the required flow; coil capacity would need a separate heat-transfer assessment.
2. The control-valve setting changes the system curve. Fixed speed provides more head, so its valve needs greater resistance to hold the required flow. Slowing the pump lowers its curve and allows a less restrictive valve setting. Each pump/system intersection sits at the same flow, with the surplus fixed-speed head dissipated across the valve.
3. One pump carries 0.8 of its reference BEP flow. The upper limit is 1.2 times speed-scaled BEP, giving a minimum speed fraction of **0.8/1.2 = 2/3**. At that speed the gross head is 9.102 m. Subtract branch loss of 1.28 m and shared passive loss of 2.88 m: the valve must absorb **4.942 m**. The flow constraint prevents the pump slowing enough to reach a fully-open valve condition.
4. P1 and P2 normally run at 70% load. Losing P3 leaves both duty pumps operating, with the spare unavailable. Losing P1 requires P3 to replace it alongside P2. Both final states achieve **120.44 m3/h**, drawing **12.883 kW fixed** or **4.900 kW variable**. Cooling through detection and startup would need a transient assessment.
5. The reference year uses **3504/3504/1752 hours at 40/70/100% load**, totalling 8760. Annual pumping energy is **95.76 MWh fixed** and **50.63 MWh variable**, a **45.13 MWh (47.1%)** reduction. The combined unfavourable case adds 10% passive/branch resistance, an 8 m terminal floor, adverse efficiencies and 90% of hours at full load. Maximum speed is needed at the dominant condition, allowing VFD losses to outweigh head reduction. Pumping energy then rises **6.96%**. Both percentages apply to the secondary pumps.
6. Real pipe/component resistance or terminal pressure demand could exceed the assumed budgets. At 25% higher passive/branch resistance, two pumps achieve just **164.51 m3/h**, short of full duty. Seek actual pump curves, pipe/valve/coil pressure-loss data and critical-path DP measurements. On the thermal side, sufficient water flow can coexist with poor coil heat transfer or air distribution. Coil ratings, airflow data and rack inlet temperatures would help establish performance. Ride-through also needs pump/valve timing and thermal inventory; fill pressure and NPSH require their own hydraulic checks.
